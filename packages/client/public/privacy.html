Privacy policy

Last updated September 29, 2026

Split Signal is a browser party game. You can play without an account, and the game keeps as little about you as it can. This page explains exactly what it handles.

Playing as a guest

When you create or join a room you give a display name. The server keeps it in memory, with your team and your moves, only while the room exists; rooms are deleted a few minutes after everyone leaves. Nothing about guests is written to a database. The server's logs may briefly include display names and room codes.

Voice, clips and drawings

Voice chat connects your browser directly to the other players' browsers (WebRTC). The audio is encrypted between browsers and does not pass through the game server. If your network needs it, audio may be relayed through a TURN server run for this site, which cannot decrypt it. To set up voice connections your browser contacts a public STUN server run by Google, which sees your IP address.

Some puzzles use short recorded voice clips or drawings instead of live voice. These are sent through the game server to the other players in your room and held in memory only until they are delivered. They are never saved.

Signing in with Google (optional)

Signing in only keeps your stats: matches played and won, rounds won, and your best results for each puzzle. When you sign in, Google tells the game your Google account ID and your name, and the game stores those, your match results, and a sign-in session (kept as a secure hash). It does not store your email address or anything else from your Google account. See Google's privacy policy for how Google handles sign-in.

If you play as a guest and then sign in within 30 minutes, the results of the match you just finished are saved to your account. Otherwise they are forgotten.

Deleting your data

Signed-in players can delete their account at any time: open your name in the top bar, choose Your stats, then Delete my account and stats. This permanently removes your account, your sessions and your match results.

Your browser's storage

The game saves your display name, the rooms you are seated in (so you can get back in after a reload) and, if you sign in, your session in your browser's local storage. It uses no advertising or analytics cookies. Google's sign-in button, when shown, is provided by Google and may use Google's own cookies.

Sharing

Your data is not sold or shared with anyone, and is only used to run the game. Other players in your room see your display name, and hear or see whatever you send them in play.

Children

Split Signal is not directed at children under 13.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, this page will be updated and the date above changed. Split Signal is open source; questions can be asked on its GitHub issue tracker.
